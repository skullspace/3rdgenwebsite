# Agents.md

Skullspace is fundamentally a space for humans to gather and grow as a community of people.

Contributions to our website should reflect the primacy of people in building and maintaining it. At all times the website needs to be human readable and editable.

The use of machine assistance, generative AI and all forms of automation can be useful tools in supporting our human flourishing. The documented outputs of large language models in particular are acceptable for the code side of things (CSS) and grunt work like text re-formatting, text extraction, tagging etc.

All such technical outputs require human review to ensure they are suitable for other humans to review and edit.

To borrow from Cory Doctorow, we want to make sure these tools empower us as centaurs and avoid pitfalls like automation bias and becoming reverse centaurs.

Website copy should always be human authored, though things like feedback are as permissible as spellcheck.

Commits to our repository in any branch should be human made after human review of the suitability of the suitability of the output and include at a minimum a human authored opening commit line. Preferably if a commit requires additional explanatory paragraphs the opening number of these should be human authored too. When generative text tools are involved in technical work it is helpful to include things like prompts and chain of thought as well in commit messages where available as long as these are after the human commit message lines and the line clearly delineated.

The commit policy doesn't preclude the use of agents in a local developer repository to do multi-step work consisting of automated commits. It's possible to bring these over into the domain of human review and human involvement with git history rewriting management features like cherry-pick (-n recommended), rebase and squashes. As such, some local commits of machine origin can be transformed into human authored commits that meet policy and are acceptable for push to the repository.

If your work is substantially transformative (major changes) or if you are unsure of the quality but are ready for feedback, please push to a branch and ask for review.
